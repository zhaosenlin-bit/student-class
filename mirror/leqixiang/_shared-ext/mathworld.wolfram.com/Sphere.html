Sphere

Download Wolfram Notebook

A sphere is defined as the set of all points in three-dimensional Euclidean space that are located at a distance (the "radius") from a given point (the "center"). Twice the radius is called the diameter, and pairs of points on the sphere on opposite sides of a diameter are called antipodes.

In the standard convention, an -sphere has intrinsic dimension and lies in -dimensional Euclidean space (Hocking and Young 1988, p. 17; Maunder 1997, p. 21). The ordinary sphere is therefore a 2-sphere, denoted when its radius is 1. A circle is a 1-sphere, and a glome is a 3-sphere. Coxeter (1973, p. 125) instead indexes spheres by the dimension of the ambient Euclidean space, calling a circle a 2-sphere and an ordinary sphere a 3-sphere. This alternative convention must be considered when consulting older literature.

The term "sphere" refers to the surface only, so the ordinary sphere is a two-dimensional surface. The colloquial practice of using the term "sphere" to refer to the interior of a sphere is therefore discouraged, with the interior of the sphere (i.e., the "solid sphere") being more properly termed a "ball."
