In Schrödinger's original formulation, a cat, a flask of poison, and a radioactive source are placed in a sealed box. If Geiger counter detects radioactivity, the flask is shattered, releasing the poison, which kills the cat. The Copenhagen interpretation implies that, after a while, the cat is simultaneously alive and dead. Yet, when one looks in the box, one sees the cat either alive or dead, not both alive and dead.
