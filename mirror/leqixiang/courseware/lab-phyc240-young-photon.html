About the Photon Double-Slit Experiment

This simulation demonstrates how individual photon detections build up different statistical patterns depending on whether which-slit information is available.

  1. Photon Stream: many photons are emitted. Without which-slit detection, an interference pattern gradually develops.
  2. Single Photons: individual photons arrive at seemingly random locations, but many detections reveal the same interference pattern.
  3. With Detector: a which-slit measurement distinguishes the alternatives. In the idealized model, the interference term disappears, leaving a broad two-component distribution.

Graph: cyan shows the accumulated histogram of detected photons, while yellow shows the expected probability distribution. The graph appears only when the first photon reaches the screen.

Important: Particle paths are illustrative. In quantum mechanics, a photon with unmeasured which-slit information does not have a known classical trajectory through one particular slit. Detector mode represents ideal which-path measurement, not merely placing an inactive camera nearby.

Scale: slit separation, wavelength and screen distance are calculated using physical units, while the 3D apparatus uses an enlarged, illustrative geometry.
